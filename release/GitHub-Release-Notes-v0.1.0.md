## 1. Release Summary

WE-SIO v0.1.0 presents a proposed structural-intelligence framework that begins with a concrete indexing question—how variable-size blocks might support efficient candidate localization—and expands into a typed, evidence-aware account of **world entities**, **perspectives**, **residuals**, **adaptive intelligence units**, and **research evolution**.

The framework asks whether indexing and addressing can become an entry point to a broader intelligence cycle: locating relevant entities, resolving evidence under explicit perspective contracts, detecting unexplained residuals, generating questions and candidate measurements, validating discoveries, and relaying the resulting knowledge to other investigators or agents.

This is an **initial theory-and-specification release**. Its proposed performance advantages, scientific-discovery capabilities, and self-modeling benefits are **not established empirical results**.

> **Design principle:** Define the invariant, not the inventory. Preserve the residual, not just the answer.

## 2. What's Included

### 2.1 World Entity and typed perspectives

- A **World Entity (WE)** is modeled separately from its records, identifiers, descriptions, indices, and inferred representations.
- An **entity identity** is not assumed to be identical to a metric signature, similarity score, or database primary key.
- **Perspective descriptors** distinguish mathematical metrics, measurements, similarities, directional relations, and other evidence-bearing representations.
- Descriptors carry domain, units where applicable, provenance, uncertainty, time, version, and authorization requirements.
- Euclidean coordinates are optional: not every entity or relationship belongs in a Euclidean space.

### 2.2 WE-Hybrid Differential Tree (WE-HDT)

WE-HDT is proposed as a hybrid candidate-localization and evidence-resolution architecture. It can combine spatial and nonspatial perspectives while keeping index layout distinct from the semantics of the entity and the intelligence operations performed on it.

**Key research questions:** How can candidate completeness be guaranteed? When does adaptive partitioning outperform established indexing baselines? How does performance change with dimension, workload skew, and dynamic updates?

**Important boundary:** A k-d tree already supports nonuniform spatial partitions; variable-size blocks alone do not establish novelty or superiority.

### 2.3 Perspective-Adaptive Variable Blocks and Adaptive Variable-Intelligence Blocks

- **PAVB:** adaptive partitioning and localization under explicit perspective constraints.
- **AVIB:** adaptive allocation of intelligence operations, memory, and resources to blocks or nodes.
- Partition granularity and intelligence capacity are treated as **separate design axes**, even if they can co-evolve.
- Proposed split, merge, delegate, activate, and reindex actions require budget, validity, and rollback contracts.

### 2.4 Metric Leaf Intelligence Units (MLIU)

An MLIU is a logical, potentially lightweight intelligence unit associated with a perspective and its evidence; it is not necessarily a physical tree leaf or a heavyweight autonomous agent.

Its six proposed operations are:

1. **Locate** — identify relevant candidates and addresses.
2. **Resolve** — evaluate evidence under a typed contract.
3. **Relate** — identify justified connections across entities or perspectives.
4. **Infer** — produce qualified conclusions with uncertainty.
5. **Question** — generate accountable unresolved questions.
6. **Evolve** — propose and test constrained structural revisions.

### 2.5 Two-Ways CCC and residual-first discovery

- **Forward direction:** localization, constraint evaluation, and evidence-supported answers.
- **Reverse direction:** residual analysis, question generation, and candidate hypothesis or perspective discovery.
- Residuals are preserved with expected value, observation, context, provenance, uncertainty, and model version.
- New metric or perspective candidates pass semantic, mathematical, evidential, robustness, and governance checks before promotion.

**Boundary:** A residual may arise from noise, confounding, measurement error, distribution shift, or model misspecification. It is not automatically evidence of a new scientific law.

### 2.6 Delta semantics and Constrained Delta Evolution (CDE)

The framework separates:

- **World Delta:** a change in the modeled world.
- **Observation Delta:** a change or correction in recorded observations.
- **Epistemic / Knowledge Delta:** a revision of beliefs, hypotheses, or models.
- **Structural / Operational Delta:** a change to indices, policies, resource allocation, or execution structure.

CDE is explored as a candidate constrained state-and-Delta evolution method, complementary to WE-HDT within the **Structural Intelligence Framework (SIF)** Localization / Addressing domain.

**Boundary:** D-BTP's path-specific *No-Delta-Reuse* rule is not a universal CDE axiom. Chess provides counterexamples involving legitimate state revisits, reversible moves, and history-sensitive rules.

### 2.7 Self-modeling and collective relay

- The proposed progression **WE → Self-Modeling Entity (SME) → candidate Subjective Structural Entity (SSE)** is investigated with explicit limits.
- Self-model accuracy, calibration, and feedback are testable; subjective experience is not established by these functions.
- Multi-agent collaboration, adversarial validation, repeated execution, and relay portfolios are studied as potential collective-learning mechanisms.
- Agent agreement must not be mistaken for independent evidence.

## 6. Validation Priorities

**P0 — Semantics and correctness**

- Test identity invariants and perspective type conformance.
- Verify exact-search candidate coverage and correct update/version behavior.
- Preserve evidence provenance, uncertainty, and authorization boundaries.
- Exercise CDE path/cycle semantics, including chess counterexamples.

**P1 — Controlled comparisons**

- Compare brute force, k-d tree, fixed blocks, and proposed adaptive methods.
- Measure query recall, latency, memory, update cost, and adaptation overhead.
- Compare residual registries, reverse questioning, and hypothesis generation with ablated baselines.
- Evaluate self-model calibration against static and single-perspective alternatives.

**P2 — Discovery, relay, and external replication**

- Conduct independent, leakage-resistant validation of candidate metrics and hypotheses.
- Test multi-agent relay under correlated evidence and adversarial critique.
- Publish negative results, counterexamples, implementation artifacts, and replication packages.

See [TESTABLE-HYPOTHESES.md](TESTABLE-HYPOTHESES.md) and [VALIDATION-ROADMAP.md](VALIDATION-ROADMAP.md).

- **SIF:** localization/addressing and control-plane interfaces.

- **UIERU:** relay and collective intelligence evolution.

These are architectural relationships proposed for exploration; interoperability and comparative value remain to be tested.

## 9. How to Contribute Research

Contributions are most useful when they provide a traceable research relay:

1. State the question, target contract, and relevant perspective.
2. Identify the baseline and expected outcome.
3. Supply reproducible data, code or formal argument, and environment details.
4. Report positive, negative, and inconclusive results.
5. Link affected hypotheses, definitions, counterexamples, and decisions.
6. Propose revisions only after evaluating conflicting evidence and regression risks.
7. Preserve unresolved leftovers for subsequent investigators.

**A failed hypothesis with a reproducible counterexample is a valuable research contribution.**
